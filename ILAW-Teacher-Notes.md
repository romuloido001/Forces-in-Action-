# Teacher's ILAW Lesson Notes
## Forces in Action! — Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

**Learning Area:** Science 7  
**Suggested duration:** 60 minutes  
**Resource:** Interactive HTML lesson, learner worksheet, and supplied infographic

### I — Intentions
**Learning objectives:** By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Describe force as a push or pull and identify newton (N) as its unit.
2. Distinguish balanced from unbalanced forces in everyday situations.
3. Interpret and construct simple free-body diagrams using arrows to show direction and relative magnitude.
4. Calculate net force for two opposite forces and explain how an unbalanced force changes speed and/or direction.

**Success criteria:** Learners correctly classify examples, calculate net force with direction, interpret force arrows, and explain the resulting motion.

### L — Learning Experience
| Sequence | Suggested time | Learning experience |
|---|---:|---|
| Engage | 5 min | Ask learners for examples of pushes and pulls in school or at home. |
| Explore and explain | 10 min | Discuss force, newton, force meter, balanced forces, and unbalanced forces. Use the infographic and everyday examples. |
| Model | 10 min | Demonstrate a free-body diagram. Use the force-arrow simulator to compare 15 N right and 9 N left. |
| Guided practice | 30 min | Learners complete classification, motion prediction, net-force calculations, and free-body diagram explanation. |
| Reflect and close | 5 min | Learners complete the quiz and reflection; teacher previews next steps. |

**Differentiation and access:** Allow paired discussion before independent responses; read scenarios aloud when helpful; provide worked examples and vocabulary support; extend ready learners with a new everyday motion example and a labelled FBD. The teacher may adjust time, quantity, or scaffolds according to learner context.

### A — Assessing Learning
Collect evidence from:
- Classification and prediction activity checks
- Three net-force calculation items
- Free-body diagram written explanation
- 10-item quiz score
- Learner reflection

Use explanations and error patterns, not only the numerical score, to identify misconceptions. The quiz score is formative evidence and may be supplemented by teacher observation and oral questioning.

### W — Ways Forward
- **Remediation:** Re-teach force pairs, net force, and arrow direction using concrete classroom examples and a guided FBD.
- **Practice:** Give additional one-dimensional net-force problems and ask learners to explain the direction of the net force.
- **Enrichment:** Ask learners to observe a sport, bicycle, or moving vehicle and sketch forces acting on one object.
- **Teacher follow-up:** Review learner outputs and adjust the next lesson based on the most common errors.

### Content accuracy reminders
- Balanced forces mean zero net force; an object may be at rest or move at constant velocity.
- Unbalanced forces mean nonzero net force and a change in velocity (speed and/or direction).
- In the worksheet's falling-fruit example: gravity 8 N downward and air resistance 3 N upward give 5 N downward.
- In the worksheet's car example: 500 N forward and 500 N backward give 0 N net force.
- In the worksheet's box example: 15 N right and 9 N left give 6 N right.
